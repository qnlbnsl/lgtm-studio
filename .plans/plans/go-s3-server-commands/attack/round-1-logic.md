REVISE

Blocked command writes can occupy all five database writer slots in the smallest supported pool and prevent a healthy machine from recording its heartbeat.

## Findings

### logic-r1 01 | MAJOR | C8 | Blocked command sends can stall unrelated machines

Item 4b holds a `Store.Write` transaction across a transport write and flush, with a limit per stream. The store has five shared writer slots at the minimum pool size. A heartbeat and the link cleanup worker also need those slots. Evidence comes from `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:170`, `src/server/internal/store/pool.go:20`, `src/server/internal/store/pool.go:181`, `src/server/internal/store/pool.go:301`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-stream/plan.md:117`, and `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-stream/plan.md:235`.

Five machines with stalled readers can fill the slots. A sixth machine's healthy stream waits to record a heartbeat or times out trying to claim its own command. Each two-second timeout bounds one holder. Another group of sends can take the same slots as they are released. The single-stream deadline and fairness tests exercise neither competition for the pool nor an unrelated machine's cleanup budget.

Add a shared limit on transactions that hold a writer slot across a network send. Size it against the minimum pool and preserve headroom for heartbeat, admission and cleanup writes. Acquire the permit before committing a claim. When capacity is busy, yield the command turn and wake it on capacity release without spinning or ending the stream. Add a minimum-pool test that holds several writes against real transport flow control and observes a healthy machine's heartbeat acknowledgement and another machine's disconnect cleanup before the blocked sends are released.

Category is timing and availability. Evidence status is confirmed by reading. The command writer owns the change. Risk is 48, from severity 4 for interrupted service, fallout 3 for self-recovery, and probability 4 for shared flow-control stalls under load.

### logic-r1 02 | MAJOR | D16 | A second scan can repeatedly disconnect the client ahead of a restart

Items 3 and 7 accept distinct scan requests and dispatch by sequence even while a scan remains active. The client closes its stream when another runnable scan arrives while its scan slot is busy. A broken established stream promptly reconnects. Evidence comes from `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:152`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:153`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:222`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-client-link/plan.md:201`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-client-link/plan.md:258`, and `src/client/internal/link/commands.go:163`.

The owner requests scan A, then requests scan B and restart C while A's probe is running. B closes the connection before the client handles C. The new stream can redeliver A and then B while A is still running. The client can repeat this cycle until the earlier scan settles. During this interval the machine churns connections and the accepted restart cannot reach the code that cancels an active scan.

Define an eligibility rule for a distinct scan while an earlier scan can still be running. Keep B queued until A settles or can no longer be retried. Allow C to pass B while B is ineligible. Make settlement or expiry cause the writer to revisit any scan it deferred. Specify how that works with the cursor and the no-feed rule on result writes. Add a real-client case that holds A, enqueues B and C, and observes C arrive on the existing stream while B remains queued.

Category is timing and availability. Evidence status is confirmed by reading. The server command writer owns the eligibility rule and the orchestrator owns the combined case. Risk is 72, from severity 4 for a delayed restart and interrupted link, fallout 3 for self-recovery, and probability 6 for ordinary overlapping owner actions.

### logic-r1 03 | MAJOR | R6 | A committed claim is enough to complete an attempt that was never sent

The claim sets `sent_stream_id` before the send fence. The result predicate accepts the matching claimed attempt even when the writer discards it without entering the transport. Item 4b explicitly uses an accepted result before write entry as a winning race. The frozen contract accepts a result for the last attempt the server sent. Evidence comes from `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:101`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:153`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:170`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:172`, and `src/contracts/proto/lgtm/link/v1/commands.proto:67`.

A scripted fixture commits attempt 2 under the current stream and pauses before dispatch. Its matching result closes the row with `done`, and the send fence then discards the frame. The database says the command ran although that stream never received the attempt. The same data shape represents a successful write and a claimed attempt that was skipped at the cutoff.

Distinguish a durable claim from a result-eligible send of that attempt. Reset the send marker when assigning an attempt, record it under the fence before releasing a successful send, and require it in result acceptance. If transport or confirmation fails ambiguously, end the stream and let the next stream replay the command from its durable identifier. Make the pre-send fixture verify that a never-issued claim remains pending, and race a result against the sweep after a real send.

Category is record integrity and design. Evidence status is confirmed by reading. The claim, send and result builders own the shared predicate. Risk is 96, from severity 8 for an unrecoverable false completion in the row, fallout 6 for one machine requiring a new owner action, and probability 2 for a matching result before issue.

### logic-r1 04 | MAJOR | R10 | The inverse send and sweep test has no due candidate

The send fence admits a scan or restart only before expiry plus 23 hours. The sweep selects it only after expiry plus 24 hours. The inverse test holds the send for no more than two seconds and advances the fixture age only after releasing the send. Evidence comes from `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:110`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:170`, and `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:231`.

Hold the fenced send of a command admitted just before its cutoff. The real sweep cannot select that row during the hold, so the sweep leaves it unchanged even when the send-lock mutation is present. In the other ordering, a command claimed before the cutoff needs the same one-hour age advance before the real sweep can win. The fixture currently names no such advance. Neither ordering establishes the advertised race under one stable clock.

Inject a private sweep-only pass cutoff into the integration fixture so the same unchanged row can be due to the test sweep while the real send remains inside its own live window. Observe candidate selection and the actual nonwaiting lock refusal in the inverse case. Sweep again after release, with a real matching result winning in one run and a still-pending row expiring in the other. Fail the named mutations at those observations. Use real database transactions in both orders.

Category is test design. Evidence status is confirmed by reading. The integration test writer owns the fixture. This is a required gate, so it carries no numerical risk score.

### logic-r1 06 | MINOR | R7 | The stored expiry outcome loses its source

The table stores only `outcome` and `finished_at`. A result from the client and an unanswered row finalized by the sweep can both store `expired`. The frozen outcome gives the two sources different meanings. Evidence comes from `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:92`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:101`, `.plans/plans/go-s3-server-commands/plan.md:110`, and `src/contracts/proto/lgtm/link/v1/commands.proto:90`.

Give one command a matching expiry reply and leave another unanswered until the sweep. Their terminal rows can have the same shape, even when both finish after the 24-hour threshold. A later diagnostic cannot tell a client-reported expiry from a command whose effect is unknown because no result arrived.

Persist the terminal source alongside the disposition, or assign separate storage values to a client-reported expiry and a server timeout. Constrain which source can write each value. Assert in the result and sweep tests that the two terminal records remain distinguishable, without changing the frozen wire enumeration.

Category is record integrity. Evidence status is confirmed by reading. The migration, result and sweep builders own the stored disposition. Risk is 36, from severity 2 for unclear diagnosis, fallout 3 for one owner's inspection, and probability 6 because both ordinary expiry paths are specified.

## Answers to the attack questions

1. The registry row serializes same-machine sequence allocation, and identity values can have gaps after rollback. The cursor starts at zero, walks strictly greater sequence values, resets on approval and starts fresh per stream. Normal identity allocation gives a total order, but the schema does not enforce uniqueness if a value is supplied explicitly and an exhausted sequence would fail the enqueue. An attempt starts at one, is reused only for the first assignment, stays unchanged for duplicates on that stream and can show a wire gap after an ambiguous commit. It never wraps. A maximum-attempt scan or restart can be swept, while a maximum-attempt `SetState` may remain pending until removal or a matching result. A machine's signed database revision increases on a changed name or approval and fails the write if the database integer overflows. The database samples creation, expiry, finalization and pass cutoffs. Equality stops sends at 23 hours, delays sweeping at 24 hours and retains rows at exactly seven days. The cursor keeps a past-cutoff row skipped after a backward clock step until a new stream starts. The client wall clock supplies the separately accepted pruning margin. The one-day owner receipt horizon comes from `docs/specs/2026-11-25-go-rewrite-foundation-design.md:176`.
2. A confirmed rollback discards the owner effect, outbox, feed and receipt together. An ambiguous owner commit is resolved by retrying the same key while its receipt survives. An ambiguous claim ends the stream and a new stream rereads the durable row. Database errors refuse stream operations with `unavailable` and maintenance retries on the next pass. A timed-out send releases its transaction and leaves the attempt for a later stream. A stale presence row does not authorize work when the exact local handle is gone. Join-token expiry stays with Part A and is covered by deployed check C5. Owner-session expiry stays at the authenticated route. Early receipt pruning remains within the accepted clock limitation, and the send bound assumes no forward database clock step during the operation. The gaps are shared writer admission, the client's busy-scan recovery and a claimed attempt being accepted before dispatch.
3. Owner eligibility is missing machine → `not_found`, waiting or no exact live hold → refuse both, approved joining → restart only, and approved online → both, with saved keyed replies replayed before eligibility. Writer eligibility is missing or displaced → fence error, waiting → state notices only, approved → eligible notices and actions, same assigned stream or attempt ceiling → skip, and terminal → skip. For a nonstate pending row before the send cutoff, send and result are allowed when their other predicates match. From the send cutoff through 24 hours only a matching result can finalize it. After 24 hours a matching result or sweep can win the lock. A state notice remains outside both deadlines. Removal closes all pending rows and preserves earlier terminal results. Empty or contradictory cells are a claim with an accepted result before transport, a distinct runnable scan while an earlier scan is active, and a locked send with its own row already due to the sweep. A full writer pool also lacks a yield-and-retry case.
4. Zero attempt or a missing result is invalid from an approved current stream and ignored from a waiting current stream. An empty result identifier fails canonical validation. Unspecified or unknown outcomes become `failed`. `EXPIRED` for `SetState` becomes `failed`. An absent or unsupported action and an invalid typed payload fail enqueue. A zero cursor starts before all normally allocated sequences. A null stream means unsent attempt one. Null outcome and finish time mean pending. Waiting standing uses an empty name and revision one. The plan would benefit from fixing the legal `SetState` payload combinations explicitly when it defines the separate send marker.
5. Keyed owner retries serialize on the key before the machine row. Revocation takes its token lock first. Approval, rename, claim, send, result, replacement, disconnect and removal then serialize on the same machine row. The sweep skips a busy row and retries in a later pass. The stream handler owns its cursor and transport writes. Other machines share writer admission even when their row locks are independent. A client scan worker can complete while the owner loop receives a retry or restart. The client owner settles the receipt and chooses the current session for the reply, as specified in `.plans/plans/go-s3-client-link/plan.md:261`.
6. Item 1's separate-write and missing-wake mutations have rollback and feed assertions, while its constraint mutation needs the exact invalid terminal pair. Item 2's order mutation has the two commit orders, but its machine-filter mutation needs an outbox fixture with the same recorded stream association and a different machine identifier. Item 3's paging and early-return mutations have bounded-query and commit observations. Item 4a's deadline and canceled-entry mutations have direct controller and callback observations. Item 4b's predicate, wake and deadline mutations have direct barriers. Item 5's pending, removal and stop mutations have direct assertions, and its lock mutation needs an observed nonwaiting refusal. Items 6 and 7 largely tie mutations to route, receipt and lock observations. Item 8's hook and worker mutations have concrete route and lifecycle observations, while its sweep/send mutation needs the revised clock fixture. The surviving changes below are distinct from the named mutations unless explicitly flagged above.
7. Wrong implementations allowed by the stated assertions are item 1 accepting `attempt = 4294967296` when the constraint test checks only a terminal pair, item 2 conflating a client-reported expiry and an unanswered expiry, item 3 permanently idling after a short ineligible page even after a new enqueue, item 4a failing to clear the deadline after normal success, item 4b using one shared writer slot per blocked machine with no global cap, item 5 sweeping at exactly the 24-hour boundary or pruning at exactly seven days, item 6 enqueuing a rename notice with the previous name but the next revision when the test checks only revision and count, item 7 ignoring a restart request key if the keyed race exercises only scan, and item 8 letting an otherwise eligible restart reach its handler without a session. The production test tries anonymous actions against a waiting machine, and the existing router test samples approval alone from the Machines service at `src/server/internal/api/server_test.go:108`.
8. The data shape permits a claim to look sent, leaves the source of `expired` unrecoverable, and can represent device-produced terminal outcomes on unsent rows. The current column checks also leave sequence uniqueness implicit. A send marker constrained against the stream and attempt, source-aware outcomes, and a unique sequence key would make these states decidable or impossible. A shared admission limit would preserve writer capacity across independent machine rows.
